Beat per meter

An echo from range R is a copy of the TX chirp delayed by τ = 2R/c. Mixing it with the live TX (the LO) leaves the frequency difference between the two parallel ramps: the beat frequency. HPF corner

The IF high-pass filter is placed at the beat frequency of the minimum range. It knocks down leakage and very near clutter before they reach the ADC. It is modeled as 2nd order (40 dB/decade), which also partly compensates the 1/R4 fall of echo power. It attenuates a tone and the analog noise at the same frequency equally, but not the ADC's own noise, which is added after it.

Noise and spectrum

Phase noise model

The six phase noise boxes show the current curve at the center frequency. Behind them is a reference curve defined at 12.025 GHz (initially your measurement), joined by straight lines on log-log axes; typing into a box adjusts that reference point so the displayed value matches. The curve is split into two physical sources: the PLL in-band noise (the points up to 100 kHz, held flat beyond) and the free-running VCO (the line through the 1 MHz and 10 MHz points). The loop acts as a low-pass for the in-band part and a high-pass for the VCO, crossing at the loop bandwidth. Changing the loop BW or the in-band and out-of-band offsets recombines the two parts; a correction term makes the curve match your points exactly at the reference settings. Finally, frequency multiplication adds 20·log of the frequency ratio.

Range correlation

The echo carries the same phase noise as the LO, delayed by τ. The mixer subtracts the two phases, so slow phase wander that hardly changes within τ cancels, like common-mode noise in a differential amplifier. Fast wander does not cancel. Near objects (small τ) are cleaned up to high offsets; far objects only at low offsets. With correlation turned off, the factor becomes +3 dB (two independent sources). For the leakage, τ is tiny, so the factor grows as (2π·Δf·τ)2: every halving of the leakage path difference lowers the leakage noise floor by 6 dB.

factor = 4·sin2(π·Δf·τ)    ≈ (2π·Δf·τ)2 for Δf ≪ 1/(πτ)
Δf
offset from the object's own beat frequency, ΔR·k
τ
delay of the object whose phase noise is being cancelled, 2R/c (for the leakage: Leakage Path Diff. / c, shown as Leakage delay in Calculated)

Thermal + ADC floor

The floor in every FFT bin comes from two independent noise sources, worked out separately and then added in power. Everything is referred to the RX input so it can be compared directly with the received echo powers.

RF path. The front end adds noise like any receiver: kT at the input raised by the noise figure. Mixer and IF amplifier flicker noise adds to it at low beat frequencies (near ranges). The IF HPF then attenuates this noise exactly as much as it attenuates an echo at the same frequency.

ADC path. The ADC adds its own noise after the RF gain and the HPF. An ADC with full scale FS and a given SNR has a total noise power of FS − SNR, spread evenly from 0 to fs/2. To compare it with an RX-input signal it is divided by the RF gain, which is Friis' formula with the ADC as the last stage. The HPF does not reduce it, so where the HPF cuts deep (short range), the ADC noise can dominate.

NRF = −174 + NF + 10·log Δfbin + ENBW + 10·log(1 + fk/f) + |H(f)|2dB
NADC = (FS − SNR) − 10·log(fs/2) − GRF + 10·log Δfbin + ENBW
floor = NRF ⊕ NADC

Windowing

A window tapers the edges of the sampled chirp. It buys low sidelobes, so a strong reflector does not leak across the whole range axis, at the cost of a wider main lobe and a slightly higher noise bandwidth (ENBW), which is added to every noise term. Sidelobes of stationary clutter repeat identically every chirp, so the Doppler FFT removes them for moving targets.

Building the spectrum

For every point on the range axis, each enabled object (and the leakage) adds four things, and the yellow total is their power sum. If the point is inside the object's own bin, the object appears as a peak. Otherwise it contributes its phase-noise skirt at offset Δf = |R − Robj|·k, its window sidelobe at that distance, and any spur products.

The skirt is phase noise (dBc/Hz) times the bin bandwidth, which is why the FFT bin width appears in it. The sidelobe term is a pure processing artifact of the tone, so it uses the HPF value at the object's frequency, not at the point being evaluated.

Spurs

A spur is a discrete phase-modulation sideband at offset fs. After dechirping, every object gets two copies at its beat frequency ± fs: a ghost target, scaled by the same range-correlation factor as phase noise. A fixed spur gives sharp ghosts. A fractional spur is modeled as fixed in absolute frequency, so its offset drifts with the chirp across the window; its energy is spread over all the bins it crosses and drops by that number of bins. Negative beat frequencies fold back onto the positive axis.

ghost at Rg = |fobj ± fs| / k,   level = P + Ls + 20·log(fc/fref) + 10·log[4sin2(πfsτ)] + 20·log g

Ghosts from stationary reflectors repeat identically every chirp, land at zero Doppler and are removed for moving targets. Ghosts of a moving target move with it and survive Doppler processing.

How much the phase-noise skirts of the other objects raise the floor in the target's bin, compared with thermal and ADC noise alone. Above about 3 dB the target is phase-noise limited: more TX power raises the target and the clutter skirts equally, so its SNR stops improving.

Warnings

  • Nyquist: the beat at max. range exceeds fs/2 and would alias.
  • Previous chirp tail: for the first τ of the window, an echo still belongs to the previous ramp. Its beat is about S·(Tp − τ), usually far above the IF band, so the anti-aliasing filter must reject it.
  • Reset echo: a strong object with τ larger than the settling time returns its reset sweep inside the window. It sweeps through every IF frequency and raises a broadband floor; this is flagged but not modeled.
  • Same bin: two objects closer than about two bins are not resolved.
  • Phase-noise limited: Phase noise loss above 3 dB.
  • Ghost: a spur product more than 10 dB above the floor; possible false target.

Assumptions and limitations

  • Point objects, free space, no multipath, no antenna pattern, no atmospheric loss.
  • Reflectors are stationary and targets are moving, for the Doppler results only.
  • Real IF sampling; the AAF and its rejection are not modeled.
  • Window sidelobes use envelope approximations; the main lobe is simplified.
  • The phase noise loop split assumes points ≤100 kHz are in-band and the 1–10 MHz points follow the VCO.
  • Fractional spurs drift at the chirp slope (integer-boundary type).
